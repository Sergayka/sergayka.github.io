2026.06 — now

Product company · NDA
SRE / Platform Engineer

One template for dev, prod, and infra

Joined when the product had no platform of its own. The clusters belonged to someone else, deploys were manual, and the team heard about outages from users. The platform was built in the same months as the product work.

  • GitOps on all three environments from the start. GitLab CI, a private registry, Argo CD. The cluster converges to what is in git. Rollback is reverting a commit. Merge to a working version takes about 3 minutes.
  • Databases and queues went to operators. PostgreSQL through CloudNativePG, RabbitMQ, Valkey. Backup and restore no longer depend on one person.
  • One observability standard, on the clusters and on the nodes outside them. Four signals — latency, errors, load, saturation — and a check that the service is alive from the outside. VictoriaMetrics, Grafana, Alertmanager, Loki. Alerts cover about 99% of the incident types we see.
  • Product alerts stay separate from infrastructure alerts, so on-call isn't buried in someone else's noise. Critical pages go to PagerDuty, not only Slack. Production can wake the person on call. Dev is low urgency and stays quiet at night. The public edge holds about 99.9%.
  • What the cluster can't see is watched from outside: DNS, certificates, the proxy, 5xx at the edge. After 3 failures from 2 locations, user impact is clear in about 3 minutes.
  • Secrets live in Vault and enter the cluster through External Secrets. They are not in git and not in images. The network is closed by default, admin interfaces are limited to an address list, and the rule is in git, so a restart doesn't reopen it.
  • Superset on the same database, read-only, for six financial views: revenue, payment success by provider and method, new and repeat payments, active, new, and churned users. Analysts stopped exporting tables. Production queries stay off that path.

2024.10 — 2026.08

Fabrika Koda · Taganrog
SRE / Golang

Production, stood up while the monolith grew

Joined as the monolith was expanding quickly. Production and development had to be built, and the product work didn't stop: payments, the API, the data.

  • Redis on the critical API paths. Response times improved by about 40%, and the primary database stopped carrying that load. Idempotency for payments, auth, and order creation sits on the same cache.
  • A stack the team actually looks at. Prometheus, VictoriaMetrics, Grafana, Alertmanager, Loki, with Sentry and GlitchTip split between the old projects and the new ones. Telegram alerts are cut by project and environment. Uptime sits around 99.9%.
  • ClickHouse keeps logs for 6–12 months. The heavy entities left PostgreSQL, which freed about 50–60% of the disk. The same data became business dashboards. From those, the team proposed changes that raised the average order from $15 to $24.
  • Delivery and access, from a bare server. Proxmox on Selectel, dev / stage / prod on separate VMs, Nginx as the front door, OpenVPN for the private network. Jenkins with Bitbucket and Vault, Ansible for the machines.

Alongside that, the backend: T-Bank, Octo, Payze, and Alfa, including webhooks and refunds, plus the slow queries and indexes in PostgreSQL.

  • ~40%faster critical API
  • $15 → $24average order, from the data
  • ~50–60%disk freed off PostgreSQL

Three products, then Kafka

A year on the backend of three products at once. The services were new, and the path between them had to grow with the load.

  • REST APIs from scratch on Gin, with the services themselves on net/http. MongoDB and PostgreSQL, through migrations.

Backend, Python and Go

PHP to Go, and a network written down

Backend across the company's projects, and the first time the infrastructure was described instead of assembled by hand.

  • Django and FastAPI, and a refactor of the legacy PHP into Go.
  • Redis to Valkey for the latency-sensitive cache and queues. About 15% faster.
  • Vault for secrets. Keycloak as the single login for the internal tools: ELK, GitLab, Vault.
  • Grafana and Prometheus on prod and dev. The layout in Terraform: isolated networks, a load balancer, a hot standby.
