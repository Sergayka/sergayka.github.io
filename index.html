01 — Work

2026.06 — now

Product company · NDA
SRE / Platform Engineer · Senior

Platform for dev, prod, and infra

Joined when the product had no platform of its own: third-party clusters, manual deploys, and the team learned about outages from users. In 4 months, built dev, prod, and infra on one template while also handling the product work. Delivery, observability, and security work the same way on every environment.

Ops

  • GitOps for dev, prod, and infra from the start. GitLab CI, a private registry, Argo CD. The cluster converges to what is in git. Rollback is reverting a commit. Merge to a working version takes about 3 minutes. Before, the same thing took 20–30 minutes by hand.
  • Databases and queues went to operators, instead of containers run by hand. PostgreSQL through CloudNativePG, RabbitMQ, Valkey. Backup and restore no longer depend on one person.

Observability

  • One standard on the clusters and on every node outside them. Four signals: response time, errors, load, saturation, plus a check that the service is alive from the outside. VictoriaMetrics, Grafana, Alertmanager, Loki. Alerts cover about 99% of the incident types we see.
  • Product alerts stay separate from infrastructure alerts, so on-call isn't buried in someone else's noise. On that split, the public edge holds about 99.9%.
  • HetrixTools next to the internal monitoring, for what the cluster can't see: DNS, the certificate, the proxy, 5xx at the edge. After 3 failures from 2 locations, it is clear in about 3 minutes that a user is affected.
  • Critical alerts go to PagerDuty, not only Slack. Production wakes the person on call, push or SMS. Dev is a separate low-urgency service and stays quiet at night. The incident doesn't depend on whether the chat is open.

Security

  • Secrets only in Vault, into the cluster through External Secrets. Not in git, not in images. The network is closed by default. Only what is needed is open. Admin interfaces are limited to an address list, and internal ports are not on the internet. The rule is in git, so a restart doesn't reopen access.

Analytics

  • Superset for the financial dashboards. Before that, the money figures were exports from the database. It runs on the same database, analysts have read-only access, and production queries stay off that path. Six marts: revenue, payment success by provider and method, new and repeat payments, active, new, and churned users.

Networking

On live traffic, the whole request path: DNS, CDN, TLS, reverse proxy, load balancing, the firewall, and how a packet moves between regions. The symptom separated a DNS failure, a certificate, a stale CDN cache, or a break at the edge from the service itself being down. A network incident closed once the cause was clear, not after restarting everything.
